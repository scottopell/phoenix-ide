# Phoenix Vision

Phoenix is a personal LLM working environment, developed from the premise that LLMs account for most development and research activity. The harness used to interact with LLMs is the biggest quality of life investment that more people should be making. Coding provides the initial use case; scope should follow experience with how LLMs become useful.

**Personal fit determines direction.** Priorities come from actual use. Phoenix has one primary user, github.com/scottopell. However it is a goal that Phoenix should be straightforward to modify around individual workflows, including changes substantial enough to produce a different product. Forks such as [Kingdom IDE](https://www.craigloewen.com/blog/2026/08/28/kingdom-ide/) are an intended outcome. Generalization should earn its complexity through concrete needs.

**Reliability enables reliance.** Entrusting more work to Phoenix requires confidence that accumulated context, decisions, and results will survive interruptions. Durability and understandable recovery should reduce supervision and make returning to ongoing work routine.

**The conversation is the durable unit of work.** Transcripts, context windows, models, and providers can change within one conversation. Those transitions must preserve its identity and history while making changes in model context understandable. Compaction creates a handoff; earlier transcripts remain evidence that both people and agents should naturally consult. Repositories and execution environments are attachments to the work, leaving room for conversations beyond coding.

**The interface should support an accurate mental model of the harness.** System prompts, tool activity, retries, and compactions belong to the experience and must remain inspectable. Information density and progressive disclosure must coexist. Essential state belongs beside the work; deeper views extend that understanding. Simplification should reduce interpretation effort while preserving meaningful distinctions.

**Model and provider choice is a primary product concern.** Result quality, reliability, latency, and cost depend on that combination. Phoenix should support informed experimentation and switching without fragmenting the conversation. Relevant configuration and usage evidence belongs in the conversation, with deeper analysis available and unknown provider details represented honestly.

**Ownership should remain practical.** Self-hosting should provide direct access to records, execution environments, and the software itself. Storage and authority should be straightforward to understand. Architectural choices should favor ordinary, inspectable mechanisms and accommodate pragmatic exceptions.

The measure of progress is whether more consequential work can be entrusted to Phoenix with less supervision, while retaining the ability to understand, intervene, and change how the system works.
